# User Pain Points — HOA/Community Management Software — 2026-03-26

## Summary

Self-managed HOA boards (10–150 units) are overwhelmingly stuck using Excel and email because dedicated software is either too expensive (minimum fees that eat a double-digit percentage of dues), too complex (built for professional management companies, not volunteers), or too fragmented (accounting in one tool, communications in another, payments in a third). The software that does exist receives consistent complaints about dated UX, poor mobile apps, and lack of true workflow automation. The market gap is clear: **no dominant, affordable, all-in-one platform exists that is purpose-built for the volunteer-run small HOA.**

---

## Top Complaints (Ranked by Frequency)

1. **Pricing is prohibitive for small communities** — Per-unit minimums ($350/mo) and flat fees ($100/mo) are unaffordable for 10–100 unit HOAs. One user: *"I found some that are priced at $0.75 per unit, but with $350/month minimums."* Another: *"PayHOA seems like it will do the job but it's $100 a month and we don't generate enough money for that"* (91-unit HOA treasurer, July 2025). Even $25/month was called "a double-digit percentage of our monthly HOA dues which seems too steep" for a tiny HOA.

2. **Software built for management companies, not self-managed boards** — The primary customers of existing platforms are professional property management companies, so the UX and feature sets cater to them. Volunteer board members — non-technical, part-time, rotating — are an afterthought. One user: *"The main problem I'm seeing is that these management software, the primary customers are property management companies so they cater to them."*

3. **Too feature-rich / bloated** — Every platform reviewed includes features self-managed small HOAs don't need (e.g., condo building functionality, multi-company management, complex lease management). One user: *"The app is always far too feature-rich, overpriced (due to our unit size), or self-contained in a proprietary website."*

4. **Fragmented toolstack — no true all-in-one** — HOAs routinely cobble together: Excel for accounting, email for communications, a bank portal for dues, Facebook for announcements, Google Drive for documents. One board member: *"Town Square for communications and a bank portal for dues? That doesn't cut it in 2025."* (TX Condo, April 2025). Integrations like QuickBooks are required for basic accounting on many platforms.

5. **Poor UX / Outdated interfaces** — Existing tools feel like 2010-era software. One user: *"I've never been more frustrated with a software — even 2010 Facebook ran more efficiently — the amount of technical issues, it's very inefficient and unproductive."* Multiple complaints describe interfaces as "clunky," "antiquated," and directing users to "web-based sites" instead of a clean app.

6. **Poor mobile experience** — Apps described as "an afterthought" (Vantaca review). Homeowners on TownSq complained the app missed critical due-date emails, resulting in late fees. *"They also don't have any way on TownSq to contact the board."*

7. **Financial transparency & accountability gaps** — Homeowners and board members alike struggle to get clear financial reporting. One user: *"I'd develop anything that brings financial accountability and transparency to how HOA dues are spent, tracked, and managed."* Volunteer treasurers are manually correcting balance sheets that management companies provide.

8. **Manual dues collection / invoicing is a massive time sink** — Volunteer treasurers report sending invoices via Word, tracking payments in Excel, chasing late payments manually. One treasurer for a 91-unit HOA: *"the process of sending out yearly dues invoices takes entirely too long."*

9. **Vendor/maintenance request tracking is broken** — Work orders never followed up on, outstanding dues ignored, no centralized tracking. Horror stories about management companies: *"Ones that gave incorrect advice on outdated statutes, never did work orders, never followed on outstanding dues."*

10. **Document management is ad hoc** — HOA docs scattered across email, Google Drive, Dropbox, paper files. No single accessible repository. Records lost when board members turn over. One user: *"learn how to back up email accounts, store it on Google Drive AND Dropbox AND another backup. Expecting your property manager to do it is a fools errand."*

11. **Violation/CC&R enforcement is manual and error-prone** — Bogus notices sent to wrong residents. One user: *"You have residents complaining on our Facebook group about getting bogus notices all the time."* No automated escalation workflows.

12. **Opaque pricing — advertised price ≠ actual price** — *"Our board is reluctant to look at tools because the advertised price isn't what most HOAs pay."* Hidden fees, per-module charges, and setup costs erode trust during evaluation.

## Self-Managed HOA Specifics (10–150 Units)

**This segment is the most underserved.** Key characteristics from research:

- **Primary user:** Volunteer board member — treasurer, president, or secretary — with no property management training. They have a day job. The HOA is nights-and-weekends.
- **Current toolstack:** Excel + Word + Gmail + a bank's payment portal + Facebook Group. Occasionally Google Drive for docs.
- **Budget reality:** HOAs in this range collect $100–$500/unit/year in dues. A $100/month software bill can represent 5–15% of their entire operating budget.
- **Pricing sensitivity:** The "$350/month minimum" per-unit pricing model is a hard blocker. Even $25/month is cited as potentially too steep for tiny associations.
- **Feature needs (actually wanted):**
  - Dues collection with autopay (biggest ask)
  - Invoice generation and late fee tracking
  - Basic accounting / financial reporting for board meetings
  - Document repository (CC&Rs, meeting minutes, bylaws)
  - Violation/ARC (Architectural Review Committee) tracking
  - Community announcements / resident messaging
- **Feature needs (explicitly NOT wanted):**
  - Complex multi-property management
  - Full general ledger / QuickBooks-level accounting
  - Building amenity management (elevators, parking structures)
  - Features designed for professional management companies
- **Turnover problem:** Board members rotate every 1–3 years. Software must have zero learning curve or adoption collapses. Excel survives because everyone knows it.
- **Adoption resistance:** Boards are skeptical of software after being burned by tools with hidden fees, price increases post-signup, or tools that required homeowners to create accounts they'd never use.

## Opportunities for Kemuni

- **Price on units, not features, with no minimums.** The clearest market gap is transparent, per-unit pricing with no monthly floor — something like $1–2/unit/month that scales linearly. A 30-unit HOA should pay $30–60/month. A 100-unit HOA pays $100–200/month. Every user who said "PayHOA is too expensive" or cited the $350/mo minimum is a potential Kemuni customer.

- **Design for the volunteer, not the property manager.** The UX bar is low — nobody has nailed this. A clean, modern interface designed for a non-technical volunteer who spends 2 hours/week on HOA tasks would stand out immediately. Focus on the 5 core workflows (dues billing, announcements, docs, violations, maintenance requests) and make each one stupid-simple.

- **Automated dues collection with smart reminders.** This is the #1 manual time sink. Autopay enrollment, automated reminders before due dates, late fee calculation, and a real-time dashboard of who's paid — delivered without the treasurer doing anything — would be the killer feature for this segment. One reminder: build for the 91-unit HOA treasurer who currently uses Word to mail invoices.

- **Transparent, embedded onboarding with no lock-in anxiety.** Boards are burned by software with hidden pricing and painful migrations. A Kemuni differentiator: (1) genuinely transparent pricing shown upfront, (2) easy data export at any time, (3) a free trial that actually shows value in day 1. This directly addresses the stated fear: *"the advertised price isn't what most HOAs pay."*

- **Communication-first, not accounting-first.** Most HOA software leads with accounting (Buildium) or tries to be a social network (TownSq). Neither solves the core problem: homeowners don't know what's happening, and board members are drowning in one-off questions. A lightweight announcement feed, a resident portal where homeowners can check their payment status and submit requests without calling anyone, and a board-to-resident messaging system would immediately reduce the most complained-about friction.

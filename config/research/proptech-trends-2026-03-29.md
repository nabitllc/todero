# PropTech & HOA Software Trends — 2026-03-29

## Funding & M&A Activity

- **PropTech total investment hit $16.7B in 2025** (up 68% YoY), with AI-native companies taking a fast-growing share. AI companies accounted for ~$4.5B of that — and they're outpacing SaaS players for new funding in 2026. *(Source: BisNow via allwork.space, March 2026)*
- **February 2026 PropTech funding:** ~$1.04 billion across 38 transactions (median deal: $6.6M). Fewer deals but larger checks — signs of increased VC selectivity. *(Source: CRETI, March 2026)*
  - Top deals: Kiavi ($350M debt), Metiundo ($47.6M Series A), OneDome ($34.2M Series C), Stake ($31M Series B), Ownwell ($30M)
- **AppWork raised $7M** (led by Resolve Growth Partners) — AI-powered maintenance platform for multifamily properties, bringing total funding to $20M. Grew 200% YoY. *(Source: Tampa Bay Business Journal, March 16, 2026)*
- **Giraffe360 raised $10M (£7.5M) Series B** — proptech for digital real estate listing content/marketing. *(Source: UKTN, March 26, 2026)*
- **Three new proptech unicorns** since mid-2024 are all AI-native, including Bedrock Robotics. *(Source: allwork.space)*

Introduced "OXP Studio" as a centralized workspace to govern AI agents across a property portfolio. Positioned around "Autonomous Property Management™." *(Source: GlobeNewswire)*
- **Buildium** released the "Lumina AI suite" — AI features integrated into Buildium's existing marketplace. *(Source: ManageCasa)*
- **ManageCasa** positioning aggressively as "next-generation" HOA platform vs. PayHOA, with 2026 roadmap pointing to smart building integrations and AI automation. *(Source: ManageCasa)*
- **Effortless HOA** emerging as an independent player targeting self-managed HOAs (10–500 homes) with modern UX as an alternative to enterprise platforms like AppFolio and CINC. *(Source: effortlesshoa.com)*
- Jurny (STR-focused): Building AI agents that handle 80–90% of inbound guest inquiries + dynamic pricing agents. A preview of where HOA comms automation may head. *(Source: Jurny blog, March 2026)*

## AI & Automation Trends

- **Agentic workflows** are the dominant theme: the shift is from "AI-assisted tasks" to "AI agents that own entire workflows autonomously." Entrata's 100+ agents is the biggest signal — this sets expectations for what enterprise platforms must offer.
- Buildium (via Lumina AI) and ManageCasa both racing to add AI layers on top of existing SaaS — primarily for: automated tenant/resident screening, late fee enforcement, maintenance routing, lease/document generation, and communication drafting.
- **Guest/resident communication agents** handling 80–90% of inbound requests are becoming table stakes in STR and multifamily; HOA platforms are lagging but the pressure is building.
- Real estate firms are beginning to explore **custom-built AI tools** rather than off-the-shelf SaaS — not viable for most, but large operators are testing it. This poses a long-term threat to mid-tier SaaS.
- Key AI automation categories showing traction: maintenance dispatch and backlog reduction (AppWork), dynamic pricing (STR), predictive risk screening, financial anomaly detection.

## Market Shifts

- **HOAs are mainstream infrastructure now:** ~377,000 HOAs nationwide in 2026, covering ~80 million Americans (~1 in 3 homes). This scale is driving demand for professional-grade software even at the self-managed/volunteer-board level. *(Source: BuildingLink/CAI data)*
- **Integrated platforms are winning over niche point solutions.** Market research indicates platforms combining management, security, and IoT are consolidating share away from specialists. The pressure is toward "one platform" rather than a stack of tools. *(Source: Verified Market Reports)*
- **VC is getting selective.** January 2026 had broad early-stage activity; by February deals narrowed sharply. Investors are now favoring companies with measurable traction, revenue, and scale rather than experimental startups. *(Source: CRETI)*
- **SaaS vs. AI-native uncertainty:** No consensus on whether AI replaces SaaS or incumbents (Buildium, AppFolio, Entrata) hold their ground by layering AI on deep data moats. Both scenarios are being funded simultaneously.
- Board-level governance complexity is rising: higher insurance premiums, aging infrastructure, homeowner scrutiny on financials — all driving demand for more capable software, not just "payment portals."

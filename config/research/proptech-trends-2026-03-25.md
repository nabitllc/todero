## Technology Trends

### Agentic AI Moving into HOA Workflows
- **Vantaca's HOAi** is the clearest leading indicator: agentic AI handling invoice processing, homeowner inquiry triage, routine communications, and workflow status updates *autonomously* — not just assistance, but execution. (Source: Vantaca blog)
- **DoorLoop** being called out for "AI-powered" dues collection, work orders, and compliance tracking at affordable price points — signaling AI is expected even at low-cost tiers.
- Effortless HOA launched an **AI-powered CC&R rules assistant** for homeowner Q&A.
- Broader proptech trend: VC is specifically moving toward **AI-first companies** over traditional SaaS — "faster output, lower costs, tools that improve as they collect more data." (BisNow)

### "Systems of Action" vs "Systems of Record" Framing
- Vantaca's thought leadership is pushing a compelling framing: most HOA software is still a "system of record" (stores data) while the next wave is "systems of action" (drives what happens next via automated workflows). This is emerging as the dominant sales/marketing narrative in the HOA software segment.
- Key buyer priority signals from 2026 roundup content: **mobile-first, workflow automation, open APIs, AI anomaly detection**, and offline capability.

### Integration & Platform Consolidation
- The industry has moved from "basic software adoption" into a phase where tools are expected to share data, trigger workflows, and reduce manual steps. Buyers increasingly skeptical of point solutions. (Source: lula.life)
- **Mixed-use platforms** (handling rentals AND associations, e.g., Buildium, AppFolio) are a distinct category — but they're built rental-first and HOA features are secondary.
- Smart building tech: biometric access, visitor management systems (Spintly) getting traction in larger residential contexts — less relevant for small self-managed HOAs but coming downstream.

### Payments & Financials
- Payment software for HOAs is a hot sub-category — ManageCasa, PayHOA, and others competing on HOA-specific billing automation.
- HOA reserve study software is also a 2026 focus area (probabilistic modeling, risk-adjusted funding). Niche but signals a move toward financial sophistication.

## Opportunities for Kemuni

1. **Position as the "system of action" for self-managed HOAs** — Vantaca has coined this framing but targets professional management companies at scale. Kemuni can own the same narrative for the underserved self-managed segment (10–150 units, volunteer boards). The message: "Not just a database — a platform that tells you what to do next."

2. **AI-assisted CC&R / rules Q&A is a fast differentiator** — Effortless HOA already launched it; PayHOA hasn't. An AI chatbot that answers homeowner questions about rules/bylaws based on uploaded docs is a low-lift, high-perceived-value feature that could be a launch differentiator for Kemuni.

3. **Own the "no management company needed" positioning** — The market is flooded with tools for *professional management companies*. Self-managed boards are underserved. Kemuni's sweet spot (10–150 units, volunteer board) is explicitly NOT who Vantaca, CINC, or AppFolio are building for. Lean into this gap aggressively in SEO and messaging.

4. **Pricing window is clear: $3–$49/mo is the self-managed HOA sweet spot** — PayHOA at $49/mo is the reference price. Effortless HOA at $3/home/mo. Kemuni should price competitively in this range with transparent per-unit pricing. Revenue-sharing models (like some competitors use) create distrust with volunteer boards.

5. **Community/content strategy: seed r/HOA and similar forums** — The Reddit thread above shows real people actively seeking solutions and asking exactly the questions Kemuni can answer. Early presence in these communities (authentic, not spammy) builds trust with the exact buyer persona before launch. HOA boards discover software through peer recommendations, not ads.

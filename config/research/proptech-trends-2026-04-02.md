## Market Trends

- **365,000+ community associations in the U.S.**, housing ~74 million Americans. The market has grown 40%+ in the past two decades, yet most self-managed boards still use spreadsheets and email (ManageCasa, 2026).
- **Self-managed HOA niche is underserved.** Capterra's 2026 HOA software rankings highlight that EasyHOA bills itself as the "only affordable software built for self-managed HOAs." Platforms like Buildium, AppFolio, and CINC Systems primarily target professional management companies — not boards running themselves.
- **Top competitors in self-managed segment (per Capterra, ZipDo, EffortlessHOA roundups, March/April 2026):**
  - **HOA Express** (rated 9.7/10 on ZipDo for self-managed use cases)
  - **PayHOA** — simple online payments for self-managed communities
  - **EasyHOA** — self-described niche player for small/mid-size HOAs
  - **Effortless HOA** — purpose-built for self-managed boards (HOA-native workflows, not repurposed rental PM software)
  - **HOA Start** — recent Capterra ratings, "very user friendly," actively reviewed in 2026
- **Mobile-first is now table stakes.** ManageCasa's 2026 guide explicitly states: "If it's not mobile-friendly, it's not ready." App Store + Google Play presence required.
- **Online voting adoption is accelerating.** Community associations are deploying digital voting systems to boost board decision participation (RealEstate Business Review, 2026).
- **HOA website presence as community hub.** YouTube video published ~2 weeks ago ("Best HOA Website Builders Ranked — Top 3 in 2026") underscores that boards want integrated web presence for communication, payments, and documents — not just standalone portals.
- **PropTech market overall:** Precedence Research (published April 1, 2026) projects proptech at strong CAGR through 2035; property managers/agents hold the largest end-user segment at 42% of market share.

## AI & Technology Shifts

- **AI is no longer a feature — it's the funding thesis.** Investors are moving away from traditional SaaS proptech toward AI-first companies that can run lean, collect proprietary data, and improve with use (AllWork.Space, March 18, 2026).
- **Agentic AI entering real estate.** Engel & Völkers (April 2026) reports: predictive analytics can now identify market trends before they appear in traditional reports. "Agentic AI" (autonomous AI that takes actions, not just generates content) is entering workflows.
- **AI being used inside HOAs today** — not just in enterprise proptech:
  - Michigan HOA attorneys report boards are already using ChatGPT to draft violation notices in seconds, summarize attorney memos, and check rules consistency (Szuradelonis law blog, ~4 weeks ago)
  - **Legal risks emerging:** Use of public AI tools may waive attorney-client privilege. Ward and Smith law firm (2026) published guidance: "Don't Paste the Attorney's Letter Into ChatGPT"
  - **No state-level AI regulation specific to HOAs yet** (as of early 2026, per Michigan HOA attorneys)
- **Digital marketing trends hitting real estate:** Conversational AI, voice-driven tools, AI-generated video — reshaping how property managers attract homeowners and buyers (ZoomYourTraffic / Property Industry Eye, March 31, 2026)
- **OCR for vendor invoice management** is now a standard feature expectation in HOA platforms (per Effortless HOA feature listing, 2026)
- **IoT integration for maintenance** emerging as differentiator: F9 Productions references IoT + digital financial management in HOA context (RealEstate Business Review, 2026)

## What This Means for Kemuni

**1. The self-managed HOA niche is wide open — and competitors know it.**
The leading platforms (AppFolio, Buildium, CINC) are built for professional managers. The self-managed segment has only a handful of purpose-built players (EasyHOA, PayHOA, Effortless HOA, HOA Start, HOA Express) — most of which are small, lightly funded, and not AI-native. Kemuni's timing is right to claim this segment before a well-funded AI-first player pivots into it.

**2. AI is the wedge — but legal risk is the moat.**
HOA boards are already using raw ChatGPT to draft violation notices and summarize docs — poorly. A platform that bundles safe, HOA-specific AI (pre-trained on CC&Rs, state laws, governance templates) with appropriate guardrails would solve a real problem boards already have. The legal articles warning against public AI use are free marketing for a compliant AI-enabled HOA tool.

**3. Mobile + payments + voting = the MVP bar.**
Every 2026 buyer guide emphasizes: mobile-first, automated dues collection, and online voting are now baseline expectations. If Kemuni doesn't ship all three at launch, it won't make shortlists. These are table stakes, not differentiators.

**4. Content + SEO is an underexploited acquisition channel.**
HOA software comparison videos from 2025 still rank at the top of YouTube in April 2026. There's no Kemuni-branded content in any HOA software roundup. Publishing a "Best HOA Software for Self-Managed Communities 2026" guide — or getting into Capterra/G2 before launch — could yield organic inbound before paid acquisition scales.

**5. The funding cycle creates partnership and acquisition signals.**
With $1B+/month flowing into proptech, several adjacent players (payment processors, insurance portals, IoT sensor companies) are freshly capitalized and looking for distribution partnerships. Kemuni should identify 2-3 funded startups from the Feb 2026 cohort (Stake, Ownwell, Homeflow) to explore integrations or co-marketing — before a competitor does.

# Competitor Intel — 2026-03-26

## Summary

The HOA/property management software market is in an AI arms race heading into mid-2026, with every major player launching or accelerating AI features. AppFolio is the dominant public company, guiding to $1.1B+ in 2026 revenue on the back of premium tier adoption and resident monetization. Smaller players like Solume and Vantaca are moving fast on AI differentiation — the former just launched in late 2025, while Vantaca acquired an AI startup (HOAi) and is integrating it deeply into its platform. The overall market is projected to reach $18B by 2032 (CAGR 7.1%).

## Solume (solume.ai / community.solume.com)

**No breaking news in the last 7 days. Most recent news: November 2025 launch.**

**Background & Positioning:**
Solume launched its AI-powered HOA and condo association management platform in **November 2025** (Atlanta, GA). It's one of the most direct competitors to Kemuni at the early-stage level — both targeting board-operated communities with AI assistance.

**Key Features:**
- All-in-one platform: messaging, budgeting, compliance in one place
- **Live reserve models** — connects annual budget, reserve fund balance, and existing reserve study directly
- **AI Assistant** — interprets CC&Rs, governing documents, HOA laws, and regulations; answers procedural questions in real time
- Positioned as "the only all-in-one HOA management platform that directly connects your annual budget, reserve fund balance, and existing reserve study"

**Funding/Pricing:** No public funding announced. No pricing details surfaced in search.

**Competitive Notes for Kemuni:**
- Solume is early-stage (launched ~5 months ago) and targeting the same segment: self-managed/board-run communities
- Their reserve fund + budget integration is a sharp differentiator — worth understanding if Kemuni plans to address financials
- No evidence of property management company (PMC) targeting — seems board-focused

## PayHOA

**No significant news in the last 7 days.**

**Current State:**
PayHOA is a well-established, self-managed HOA software platform with strong review presence on Capterra, GetApp, and Software Advice. It's one of Kemuni's most direct incumbent competitors.

**Recent Feature Signals (from review responses):**
- Planning **collections enhancements** for early 2026 — easier filtering and workflows for late accounts
- **Petition and ballot/voting** features recently added or expanded
- Integrates with banking systems to reduce manual transaction entry
- Features include: violation tracking (CC&R enforcement), digital request workflows, secure document storage, online voting, homeowner portals

**Pricing:** Starts at a per-unit model; considered affordable for small-to-mid HOAs.

**Review Sentiment:** Generally positive. Users praise ease of use, support responsiveness, and cost savings vs. hiring a management company. Common complaints: bank integration friction, limited collections workflow sophistication.

**Competitive Notes for Kemuni:**
- PayHOA is the incumbent in self-managed HOA software — the brand Kemuni will most often be compared to
- Their weakness is collections/financials UX and limited AI — openings for Kemuni
- Their strength is brand trust built over years of positive reviews — Kemuni will need a strong review strategy early

## Key Takeaways

- **Solume is the closest early-stage competitor to Kemuni** — both are AI-native, board-focused, launched in the same era. Solume's reserve fund integration is their sharpest differentiator; Kemuni should have a clear answer to how it handles community finances.

- **AI is now table stakes, not a differentiator** — Buildium (Lumina AI), Vantaca (HOAi/Scout), AppFolio (premium AI tier), and Solume (AI Assistant) are all AI-branded. Kemuni's advantage must be in *execution quality* and *segment fit*, not just the AI label.

- **Vantaca is going hard on content marketing** — publishing buyer guides and category-framing blog posts in Feb/March 2026. If they're targeting search for "HOA software 2026," Kemuni needs to be in that conversation early, even pre-launch.

- **AppFolio's March 18 outage shows reliability remains a real gap** even for incumbents — positioning Kemuni on uptime/reliability could resonate with boards burned by outages from larger platforms.

- **PayHOA's collections weakness is Kemuni's opening** — if Kemuni can offer smoother delinquency workflows and financial automation, it's a credible switching reason for boards currently on PayHOA.

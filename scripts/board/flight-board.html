Scope moved on 25 Aug. Todero is no longer only a build tool — it must also run the project it built: customers, orders, conversations. Three channels were added for that, and one for the post-MVP multi-tenant goal. All four start at zero, so the honest effect is that the finish line got further away, not closer.

Solid bar is where Todero is now; the ghost behind it is where it started; the pin is the goal it has to meet or beat. Each goal names the tool and the feature it is measured against. These are judged, not measured — a fresh blind panel re-scores all 19 at Wave 8, and that is the number that decides whether the program loops.

Decisions

0 awaiting your veto

Design calls I have made rather than asked about, so the work can continue — each with the reasoning and the tradeoff, so you can overrule one cheaply. A decision that lands changes the design canvas too; the two must never disagree.

Infraaccepted
Own destination, folded into Settings, or something else?
RecommendationSplit it. The signal — is anything degraded right now — becomes a permanent one-line strip on Now. The detail — service list, deploy history, breaker controls, host facts — lives in Settings.
Infra is checked reactively, when something already feels wrong. A tab makes you go look; a strip answers "is anything wrong" without the trip, and only earns your attention when the answer is yes. Folding the whole thing into Settings, which is what the canvas currently shows, buries the half you would actually use.
TradeoffIf you find yourself opening Infra several times a day rather than reacting to it, the strip is too thin and it deserves its own destination. You use it more than I do — this is the call I am least sure of.
What changesWave 6 — Now gains the status strip; Settings gains the Infra detail section.
Same split-not-move applied to Calendar (due dates → Work, job timing → Settings) and Activity (feed → Now, drill-down → Runs).
Chataccepted
A destination you navigate to, or an overlay you open over whatever you are looking at?
RecommendationMake it an overlay, opened from anywhere with a keyboard shortcut and a persistent affordance, carrying the current screen as context. It leaves the tab list.
You ask about the thing you are looking at. As a tab, asking means leaving that thing — you lose the issue, the run, the agent you were mid-thought on, and have to describe it from memory instead of pointing at it. An overlay keeps the context and can pass it to the model automatically.
TradeoffChat has real furniture — conversation history, fork, export, slash commands, an agent selector. Some of that is awkward in an overlay and wants a full screen. Mitigation: the overlay expands to full screen on demand, so the history lives somewhere without costing a permanent nav slot.
What changesWave 6 — Chat leaves the nav and becomes a ⌘J overlay. Nav lands at six. Revisit after Michael uses it.
This revises the design canvas, which currently draws Chat as the sixth tab.
Credential custodyaccepted
Which credentials may Todero hold, and which stay with Michael?
RecommendationTodero never holds the credential that could revoke its own credentials. It gets delegated, scoped, revocable tokens only — never the account that issued them. Wompi, the domain registrar, and every root/owner account stay with Michael.
Custody follows blast radius and reversibility. A leaked read token is an inconvenience; a leaked payment credential moves money and cannot be undone. And the rule has a practical test: for every credential Todero holds, Michael must be able to kill it from somewhere Todero cannot reach.
TradeoffMore setup — sub-accounts, a sending subdomain, scoped tokens — instead of pasting one key per service. That friction is the feature.
What changesWave 6 — the vault becomes real (CONNECTIONS_ENCRYPTION_KEY set, encryption actually called, per-credential revocation) as a PREREQUISITE for Wave 7.
Todero’s own record on secrets is poor: a service_role key sat in 70 commits, and .env.local holds 16 plaintext keys today. lib/encryption.ts implements AES-256-GCM but CONNECTIONS_ENCRYPTION_KEY is not set and only one route ever calls it.
The ads agentaccepted
Can an agent manage ad campaigns and budget autonomously?
RecommendationNo — it proposes, you approve, then it executes under a cap it cannot raise. The spend limit lives on the ad platform sub-account, set by you, where Todero has no permission to change it.
Ad spend is irreversible, outward-facing, and optimises for a business outcome a model will Goodhart — chasing clicks that do not convert. This is the same draft-approve-send shape as WhatsApp, and it is what finally makes Todero’s supervisor ceilings load-bearing: for ad dollars, not tokens.
TradeoffSlower than letting it run. You are in the loop on every campaign and budget change.
What changesWave 7 — propose→approve→execute is built before any ad account is connected.
Same custody rule: Todero holds the sub-account token, never the Meta/Google Business root that issued it.
What to do first for Amazoniicoaccepted
Point agents at the website, or somewhere else?
RecommendationNeither, first. Confirm Wompi with one real test transaction, and get Lina posting. Those two are hours of work and they tell you whether you have a sellable store and any traffic. Then build the order-to-fulfilment pipeline.
Of the five blockers you listed, only one is mainly code. A swarm of agents on the front-end produces a beautiful storefront and no revenue if payment is unverified and nobody is visiting. The fulfilment pipeline — order in, pack-and-ship instructions out to the employee in Colombia — is the highest-value thing Todero can build, and it is genuinely ‘manage the project’ rather than ‘build the project’.
TradeoffThe website stays imperfect while you validate payment and traffic. That is the right order anyway.
What changesWave 7 opens with the fulfilment pipeline. Storefront last. Wompi test and Lina are Michael’s, not Todero’s.
The two prerequisites are yours to do, not Todero’s — a test transaction and a conversation with Lina Menesses.

Method

How a piece gets certified

Cheap checks first. A piece is built, then the deterministic harness runs. Only when the script is green does a frontier critic look at it — and it judges only what a script cannot: whether the result is honest, and whether it beats the named comparator. Wave 2 skipped this layer and spent frontier tokens answering questions curl could have answered.

The grader is written by the orchestrator. Never by the builder being graded. A loop optimises whatever signal it is given, and a builder that writes its own check learns to write one it already passes.

Real loop bounds. Each piece gets at most 3 rounds, and halts early after two rounds with no improvement rather than burning the third. These are for loops in the orchestration script, not instructions in a prompt — a prompt-level stop is documentation of intent, not a control.

Nothing is certified twice. A piece that passed once is re-verified by every later wave. One fix landed, was verified, and was silently reverted an hour later; the harness now catches that class in seconds.
